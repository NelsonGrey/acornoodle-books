# Acornoodle Books Social Media Strategy

## Audience

Market to adults who select and purchase books: parents and grandparents of children ages 5–9, elementary teachers, homeschool families, librarians, and gift buyers. Do not build the social strategy around direct engagement with young children.

## Recommended channel order

### 1. Pinterest — primary discovery channel

Best for evergreen, searchable visual content that can continue sending visitors to individual book pages. Create boards around coloring activities, dinosaurs for kids, bugs for kids, working vehicles, rainy-day activities, screen-free learning, classroom resources, and gift ideas.

Initial cadence: 3–5 useful pins each week, produced in batches from existing covers, sample pages, facts, and activity ideas.

### 2. Instagram — primary brand and launch channel

Best for showing the covers, page details, short coloring demonstrations, new-title announcements, and the Acornoodle mascot. Aim content at parents, educators, and gift buyers.

Initial cadence: 2 feed posts or carousels and 1 short Reel each week. Reuse the strongest material in Stories during launch periods.

### 3. Facebook — supporting trust and community channel

Useful for an official business presence, launch announcements, parent and local-community sharing, and carefully selected teacher, homeschool, library, or neighborhood groups when their rules permit promotion.

Initial cadence: 1–2 posts each week, largely adapted from Instagram. Do not create a high-maintenance Facebook group at launch.

### 4. YouTube — optional second phase

Use only if there is capacity for repeatable short video: page flips, time-lapse coloring, drawing facts, and 20–40 second topic explainers. Content and descriptions should address adults choosing activities, even when the visuals appeal to children.

## Do not prioritize at launch

- TikTok: potentially useful later, but it requires a demanding video cadence and carries added sensitivity for a brand serving children ages 5–9.
- X, Threads, Bluesky, or LinkedIn: weak purchase and discovery fit for this product compared with the recommended channels.
- Snapchat: poor fit for reaching adult purchasers and for evergreen book discovery.

## First-month content system

Use one weekly theme and adapt it across channels:

1. One cover or title spotlight.
2. One interior page or fact spotlight.
3. One practical parent or teacher use case.
4. One short coloring or page-flip video, when available.

Every post should do one of three jobs: help an adult discover the book, demonstrate why it is child-friendly, or send the adult to the website or Amazon listing. Avoid filler posts and engagement bait.

## Child privacy guardrails

- Do not collect names, email addresses, photographs, voices, locations, or other personal information from children.
- Do not ask children to submit finished pages or participate in comments, contests, or direct messages.
- If featuring a child or a child’s artwork, obtain and retain documented permission from a parent or legal guardian.
- Keep newsletter sign-up, purchases, contact, and social calls to action explicitly adult-facing.
- Reassess privacy requirements before adding analytics, advertising pixels, forms, giveaways, accounts, or user submissions to the website.
